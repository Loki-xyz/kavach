## WINNING PATTERNS

### Problem Selection (Most Critical)
1. Winners target PAINFUL, SPECIFIC legal problems
2. Not generic "AI for law"
3. Access to justice for vulnerable populations
4. Revenue/speed impact for firms
5. Contract intelligence (universal workflow)

### Team Composition
- Every Stanford winner had law + CS + business
- Linguistics was a differentiator
- Judges reward domain expertise, not just tech

### Demo Requirements
- Working prototype is MANDATORY
- Slide decks lose
- 48-hour format forces functional evaluation

### Features That Win
1. Automating specific multi-step legal workflows
2. Contract reasoning/logic processing
3. Conflict detection/risk surfacing
4. Pricing/speed-to-revenue
5. Vulnerability/access angle

### Presentation Style
- Narrative: "We solve [specific problem] for [specific user]"
- Quantified impact: Time saved, dollars recovered
- Ethics awareness: hallucination risk, confidentiality, bias
- Business model clarity: SaaS/freemium
- Handle tough Q&A: data security, legal liability, go-to-market

### Technology Stack
- LLM-powered (every winner)
- Not just wrapping ChatGPT — RAG, contract parsing, logical reasoning
- Transparency: "showing its work" matters

---

## KEY TAKEAWAY

Winning demos combine:
1. A REAL painful problem
2. A WORKING prototype
3. An INTERDISCIPLINARY team
4. Clear business narrative
5. Quantified impact

The winners don't demo AI capabilities — they demo SOLVED PROBLEMS.
